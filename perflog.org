/Update/: The above was because of insertion sort. Changing it to a simple running minimum gets us to an almost 3x reduction.

We tried reducing the size of the type field in =node= to 8 bits and making the struct packed, but this led to slowdowns across the board. This is most likely due to the requirement of misaligned cache accesses not sufficiently improving any cache-bound behavior. Behavior was more neutral on the larger silk/square cases.

We ran the program without multithreading to better undrestand the TMA behavior. Retiring typically looks good, with the second-strongest category being frontend bound. This may be due to program size being too large to fit in the L1I cache.

Valgrind cannot be used on the VMs if =march=native= is used as a GCC flag. We temporarily disabled it to test memory behavior.

Between the vectorization of =MSE= and =logLoss= parallelization, we realized that =-ffast-math= turns on =-ffinite-math-only=, which prevents NaNs from being handled correctly. Though we did not observe adverse effects, to be safe we turned that specific flag off. Most of the improvements on =cancer= were earned back with the multithreading of that calculation. Turning this flag off minimally impacted the other testcases.

We initially tried to do a similar multithreading on MSE computation, but realized that vector intrinsics alone were faster, likely due to the overhead of thread fork/joins.

After our final optimization, swapping loop stationarity, we disabled =-fno-finite-math-only= to test our "maximum unsafe performance". Doing so leads to massive speed increases across the different testcases, with no big differences in output across most of them. We did notice that on =cancer= specifically, disabling this flag led to our solver finding a /better/ solution. It's unclear whether this is a floating point arithmetic error or genuinely a better program, but we reiterate that enabling =-ffast-math= without disabling this flag can lead to correctness errors on untested inputs.
